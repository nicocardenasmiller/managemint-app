A task and project planner for PhD work

Say a task out loud when it comes up and it lands in the right project with the right date. See all your projects on one timeline, and get anything with a date in Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Outlook.

Why it helps with a PhD

PhD work is a few big projects with loose deadlines, plus a steady stream of small tasks that show up in meetings, emails and hallways. Managemint is built for that mix.

Capture tasks quickly

Leaving an advisor meeting, say "send Maria the revised lit review by Friday, and the APSA deadline is January 15." You get two tasks, each with its date and in the right project.

Due dates and deadlines

When you plan to do something and the last day it can be done are kept separate, because a lot of academic work happens in the gap between them.

One timeline

Chapters, papers under review, teaching and the job market on one screen. Drag things when they move.

Templates for common projects

A dissertation chapter, a conference submission or quals prep starts with the usual steps, dated back from the deadline.

On your calendar

Anything with a date shows up in a separate "Managemint" calendar in Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or Outlook, which you can hide whenever you like. No Google sign-in needed.

Your data stays yours

It runs in your own free Cloudflare account. Nobody else, including me, can see your tasks. No ads, no tracking.

Get started

Each person runs their own copy of Managemint in their own free accounts. Setting it up takes about 15 to 20 minutes on a laptop, mostly creating accounts. You don't need to write any code or use a terminal.

  1. Create three accounts

    • A free GitHub account. Your copy of Managemint lives here.
    • A free Cloudflare account. Your server runs here.
    • An AI key. For now this is an Anthropic API key from console.anthropic.com: add $5 of credit, create a key, and set a monthly spending limit under Settings → Limits. Typical use costs well under $1 a month.

  2. Deploy your server

    Click the button and sign in to Cloudflare and GitHub when asked. Cloudflare copies Managemint into your GitHub, creates your server and database, and asks for two values:

    ValueWhat to enter
    SETUP_PASSWORDA password you make up (8 or more characters). You'll use it on your setup page.
    ANTHROPIC_API_KEYYour AI key from step 1.

    Deploy to Cloudflare

    When it finishes, Cloudflare shows your server's address. It ends in .workers.dev, for example managemint.yourname.workers.dev. Keep it somewhere handy.

  3. Open your setup page

    Go to your server's address followed by /setup and enter your setup password. From there you can:

    • Check that your AI key works.
    • Add your calendar. Tap Subscribe in Apple Calendar, Add to Google Calendar, or copy the link into Outlook. Your dated tasks then appear in a calendar called "Managemint". It's a private link that only goes one way, and Google Calendar can take up to a day to show changes.
    • Pair your phone (see below).
    • Open Managemint on your laptop.

Get the app

Set up your server first. Then install the app on your phone and pair it with a code from your setup page.

Open your server

Enter your server's address to open your web app or setup page. This browser remembers it; nothing is sent anywhere.

Download for Android
  1. Open the downloaded file. If Android asks, allow your browser to install apps, then tap Install. Android shows this warning for any app that doesn't come from the Play Store.
  2. Open Managemint and tap Scan pairing code.
  3. On your laptop's setup page, choose Pair a device and scan the QR code it shows.

Older versions and release notes are on the releases page. When there's a new version, the app tells you in Settings. Download it from here and install it over the old one; your tasks stay.

On iPhone, Managemint is a web app that your own server provides. You add it to your Home Screen and it opens like any other app.

  1. In Safari, open your server's address followed by /app/. If you saved your address above, tap Open the web app.
  2. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open Managemint from your Home Screen and enter a pairing code from your laptop's setup page (Pair a device).

Voice capture can be unreliable in Home Screen web apps on iPhone; typing a task works fine. A proper iPhone app may come later.

The laptop version, with the timeline, is also provided by your own server.

  1. On your setup page, choose Open Managemint. It's already signed in on that browser.
  2. To get it in its own window, install it. In Chrome or Edge, use Install Managemint in the address bar or menu. In Safari on a Mac, use File → Add to Dock.

Optional: connect an AI assistant

If you use an AI assistant app that supports custom connectors (also called MCP), you can let it see your tasks, add new ones and add checklists to them. For example: "what's due this week?", "turn this email from my advisor into tasks" or "break my literature review task into a checklist".

  1. On your setup page, under AI assistant, copy the connector address. It's your server's address followed by /mcp.
  2. In your assistant app, add a custom connector (usually under Settings → Connectors) and paste the address. Leave any client ID or secret fields empty.
  3. A Managemint page opens. Check that it names your assistant app, enter your setup password and choose Allow.
  4. Ask the assistant about your tasks. Tasks and checklist items it adds show up on your phone, laptop and calendar.

The assistant can find and read your tasks, add new tasks, and add, tick and rename checklist items. It can't delete anything or change tasks that already exist. You can remove its access at any time on your setup page.

Updates and feedback

Your copy doesn't update by itself. When there's a new version, the app says so. Open your copy of Managemint on GitHub, go to Actions → Update Managemint → Run workflow; Cloudflare then updates your server within a few minutes. On Android, also download the new app from this page.

If something breaks or is missing, open an issue on GitHub or tell Nico directly. Issues are public, so leave out anything private.
